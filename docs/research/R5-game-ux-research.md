# R5 — Game UX: what makes a game comfortable, simple and clear

Researched 2026-09-25 (web, four parallel reviews) at the owner's request: "research about game design (main page, arena selector, components, building, 3D…) … learn about all popular games' design tactics … and apply them to our game". This is a research appendix: what was applied lives in [03-game-design.md](../03-game-design.md), [04-technical-design.md](../04-technical-design.md) and [11-roadmap.md](../11-roadmap.md). No public A/B tests of game menus were found; the evidence is platform guidelines (Xbox XAG, Game Accessibility Guidelines, WCAG, Nielsen Norman Group), published studies, developer write-ups and playtest reports. A game named below shows what it does, not proof that it tested better.

## 1. Readability and comfort: numbers to design to

| Topic | Target for CoreEngine | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Body text | ≥ 18 px at 1080p (36 px at 4K). Laid out at 1280 × 720 and scaled × 1.5, that means ≥ 13 px authored for the smallest labels, 14–15 px for body text | XAG 101 |
| Titles | 18–22 px authored; only text ≥ 36 px at 1080p counts as "large" (may drop to 3:1 contrast) | XAG 102 |
| UI scaling | Scale with the screen, and let players reach 200 % of the minimum; a UI-scale option of 80–150 % to start | XAG 101; GAG "allow interfaces to be resized" |
| Contrast | 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for large text, control edges and focus; measured against the lightest area that shows through a see-through panel | XAG 102; WCAG 1.4.3 / 1.4.11 |
| Panels over the 3D scene | A plain dark plate behind text: alpha ≥ 0.9 (0.95 for dense text); blur is optional and does not fix contrast by itself | GAG; XAG 102; Microsoft acrylic guidance |
| Mouse targets | ≥ 24 × 24 px authored (36 px at 1080p); ≥ 44 px at 1080p for primary actions; icon plus label | WCAG 2.5.8 / 2.5.5; NN/g Fitts's law |
| Response | Feedback within 0.1 s; a busy cue after 1 s; progress after 10 s | NN/g response times |
| Animation | ~100 ms feedback, 150–300 ms transitions; 400 ms+ feels slow; a reduce-motion option | NN/g; Material; XAG 117 |
| Tooltips and hotkeys | 0.5 s delay, ≤ 5 words, hotkey in brackets ("Rotate (R)"); never the only place for essential information | Windows UX guide; NN/g |
| Colour | 8–10 % of men have red–green colour blindness: never colour alone — every status gets a symbol and a word | GAG; XAG 103 |
| Choices | Working memory holds 3–5 items; at most 2 levels of hidden options; ≤ 3 new things at once | Cowan 2010; NN/g; Hodent |
| Localisation | Allow +30 %; short English labels (≤ 10 characters) can grow 2–3× in Russian; let labels wrap to 2 lines | W3C (IBM table); LocalizeDirect |

The Unity UI Toolkit setting that follows: `ScaleWithScreenSize`, reference 1280 × 720, `Expand` (the logical canvas never gets smaller than 1280 × 720, so 16:10 and ultrawide screens get room rather than less); `PanelSettings.scale` becomes the player's UI scale.

## 2. The main screen and the arena picker

1. **One dominant Play action.** The eye goes to the biggest, most saturated element (NN/g visual hierarchy; an eye-tracking study of a games-portal menu found players hunting for where to go). Valorant 5.08 made Play bigger and red; Brawl Stars' Play is big and yellow. → START stays top-centre-right, is the only saturated green, and names where it goes.
2. **Show the destination as a picture next to START, not a dropdown.** A dropdown with few options hides what people want to compare (NN/g); Apex puts its mode selector right above Ready; Rocket League rebuilt Play as cards. → an arena chip (thumbnail, name, "Change") joined to START; a click opens the picker.
3. **One click to play.** GAG: start the game without layers of menus; NN/g found teens far less patient than adults; Roblox and Fortnite show a recent row. → the Garage opens with the last robot and arena chosen; **▶ Test** on the Build, Wire and Code screens goes straight to the arena (XAG 112: the same way back and forward on every screen).
4. **Name the problem and the fix; never block play.** Nielsen's heuristics and NN/g error-message guidance: say precisely what is wrong, next to it, with a way to fix it. Hodent: players learn by doing, so a faulty robot may fail visibly. → "Tips onto its front · Fix" instead of "1 TO CHECK"; Fix opens the screen where it is fixed.
5. **Arena cards: picture first, few facts.** Pictures are remembered better than words, with a label (NN/g picture superiority, cards); Trackmania's grid, Gears 5's mode tiles (picture, title, one sentence). → cards with a real render, the name, a one-line goal, "Needs: distance sensor ✓/✗" checked against the chosen robot, difficulty as a word and pips; easiest first; at most 6 cards; at most one "coming soon" tile.
6. **Label every picture and order actions as the work goes.** Few icons are understood without a label (NN/g); Fortnite's playtests misread a trap icon. → the action cards as a numbered workflow ("Make it work": 1 Build, 2 Wire, 3 Code, 4 Check) and a smaller "Make it yours" group (Body, Paint), with a "Next" tag on the first unfinished step.
7. **Clear the top bar.** Every visible control costs decision time (Hick's law; The Division's playtests: unnoticed filters). → languages move to a first-launch picker and Settings, each written in its own language (Smashing Magazine), not EN/O'Z/RU codes.
8. **Keep the familiar layout, disclose details on request.** Rocket League's reshuffle angered veterans; World of Tanks 2.0 buried stats and was criticised. → robot in the centre, card on the left, robots along the bottom; the card leads with status and three key numbers.

## 3. Building, parts and wiring

1. **Centre of mass and support area** (KSP's CoM/CoT/CoL markers; Trailmakers' Forces tool; Webots' support polygon, green inside and red outside). The single rule a child can follow: keep the dot inside the patch. → a Balance view in Build, on by itself when the balance check says the robot tips.
2. **An engineer's report with problems pinned in place** (KSP Engineer's Report; Tinkercad Circuits' alert icons on the faulty part; Satisfactory's red preview with a reason). → a checklist with "Show me" and "Fix", and a warning dot on START that never blocks.
3. **One-key test and instant return** (Besiege and Poly Bridge: Space; Tinkercad Circuits' Stop Simulation; Robocraft 2 wants Test Mode transitions "as near instantaneous as possible"; creative-tool research: "make it easy to try things out, and then backtrack").
4. **An X-ray while testing** (Poly Bridge's stress view; Algodoo's forces; Webots' contact points; Logic World's wires glowing when HIGH). → contact dots, green rolling and red scraping, and a slow-motion option.
5. **Ghost preview coloured by validity** (Factorio ghosts; Satisfactory blue/red with a reason). → a carried part shows green or red ("overlaps L298N"), and the centre-of-mass dot moves with it.
6. **Mirror mode** (KSP symmetry, Robocraft, Trailmakers). → M mirrors placing, moving and deleting a motor with its twin.
7. **Familiar keys and named snaps** (Tinkercad: Shift 45°, arrows nudge, D drops to the workplane; Blender's red/green/blue axes; SketchUp's named inferences). → Tinkercad's keys, and labels such as "centred on plate" while dragging.
8. **A catalogue sorted by job, with search** (KSP by function; Tinkercad Circuits' Basic/All and search, found easy by teachers in a usability study; Minecraft's search tab; Factorio's pipette Q). → groups Brain / Motors & wheels / Supports / Sensors / Power / Lights & servo, search with synonyms ("sonar" → HC-SR04), a Recent row.
9. **A part card that says what it needs** (KSP's extended tooltips; Factorio's grouped tooltips; Shenzhen I/O's datasheets). → picture, one-line job, mass, volts and amps, a mini pinout, "needs" and "used with", one tip ("Two wheels? Add a ball caster").
10. **Hotkeys on every button; advanced tools hidden at first** (NN/g recognition over recall; Besiege ships advanced building off by default; Spore: "something cool in three clicks").
11. **Starters and modules** (Tinkercad Circuits' Starters; Factorio blueprints). Starter robots are examples, which the owner ruled out (docs/13 D16); modules (a drive module: two motors, driver, caster, wires) are open to discuss.
12. **Wiring aids** (Fritzing's red rings on unconnected pins and "1 of 2 nets routed"; Tinkercad Circuits' wire colours; Logic World's peg shapes; Besiege highlights what a key drives). → after a pin is picked, light the sensible targets; red rings on required pins still empty; "14 of 16 wires — ENB, ECHO missing".

## 4. Onboarding, learning and motivation (within the pure sandbox, ADR-0008)

1. **A robot driving within 10 minutes.** Factorio rebuilt its tutorial when newcomers needed 30–45 minutes to reach automation (FFF #241); in the complex game Foldit, tutorials raised play time by up to 29 % and progress by 75 %, in simple games they did nothing (Andersen et al. 2012, 45,000 players).
2. **Teach by doing: ≤ 8 words, point in 3D, never lock the UI** (George Fan, Plants vs. Zombies, GDC 2012; Hodent, GDC 2016: ≤ 3 new things at once; forced steps showed no benefit).
3. **Hints at the moment they apply**, triggered by the player's own robot, once each (Factorio's tips failed when shown before they mattered, FFF #208, and were rebuilt to unlock by action, FFF #361; Minecraft unlocks a recipe when you pick up an ingredient). → the balance check, +5 V on VIN, a motor stall, a brown-out, a sketch never uploaded.
4. **The "why it broke" card at the failure** (Tinkercad's LED explosion and hover; KSP's flight log and Engineer's Report; only 31 % of players ever used a help button, Andersen). Plain-language compiler messages reduced repeated errors (Becker 2016).
5. **Cheap, reversible failure after a first success** (KSP's revert; productive failure g = 0.36 across 53 studies, but children in grades 2–5 did better when taught first, Sinha & Kapur 2021).
6. **Build–test–fix in seconds** (Scratch runs blocks on click; Poly Bridge's Space; feeling capable and in control predicts enjoyment, Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski 2006). → code edit to moving robot ≤ 10 s; wiring change to arena ≤ 30 s.
7. **Arenas that teach silently, ordered easy to hard, chosen by the player** (Portal's teach–test–twist; Jenova Chen: challenge works best when chosen).
8. **Optional ideas that support building, not missions** (Minecraft advancements; Turing Complete's optional goals; side goals unrelated to the main activity made 4× as many players quit early, Andersen 2011). An "Ideas" pegboard needs the owner's OK.
9. **Celebrate what the robot did, then let players share it** (Self-Determination Theory; Poly Bridge GIFs; Scratch remixing; little praise, no rankings, Shute 2008).
10. **The Notebook as an engineer's tool** (TIS-100's manual; Shenzhen I/O's datasheets; worked examples beat unguided search for beginners, Kirschner et al. 2006).
11. **Long PC sessions, not daily streaks** (GameAnalytics 2026: PC median day-1 retention ≈ 7 %, median session ≈ 18 min). → on launch, "Continue Robot 3: last run stalled at the wall".

**Avoid:** long text before play; forced or unskippable steps; tips before they matter; several message channels at once; punishment during onboarding; a help button as the main help; unrelated collectibles; rankings, generic praise and pop-ups in the middle of a run.

## 5. What was applied first (2026-09-25) and what is next

Applied: the UI scales with the screen (1280 × 720 reference, Expand); a 13 px font floor; opaque panels and card backdrops; a darker START green (5.4:1); 24 px colour swatches and wider scroll bars; **▶ Test** (F5) on the Studio's and the code editor's bars; an arena chip beside START and a picker of arena cards with real renders, goal, needs and difficulty; warnings on the robot card with a Fix button; the Balance view in Build; R to restart a run; a Settings panel with the language and the UI size. See [11-roadmap.md](../11-roadmap.md) for what was measured.

Next, in order: the workflow numbering and "Next" tag on the action cards; hints at the moment of failure in the arena (≤ 8 words, once, with Why?); wiring aids (valid targets, red rings, "x of y wires"); part cards with job, specs and needs, job groups and search; the mirror mode and hotkeys on every button; a test drive from Build with instant return; the first-10-minutes tutorial ending in a drive; the "why it broke" cards at the failure. Ideas cards and per-part test sketches need the owner's decision (docs/13).
